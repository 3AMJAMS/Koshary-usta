# Egyptian Koshary Restaurants: A Field Guide for Game Designers

## TL;DR
- Koshary shops in Egypt fall into three visual archetypes you should model: the multi-storey downtown legend (Abou Tarek, El Tahrir), the neighborhood chain (Sayed Hanafy, El Omda, Gad, Zezo), and the design-forward modern reinvention (Zooba, Ward Koshary); the signature ritual is a "maestro" (الأسطى) standing at a straight stainless-steel counter, holding a bowl in one hand and rapidly flicking scoops from adjoining metal vats with a long spoon while banging it on the pot rims (the "koshary symphony").
- The slang term "ورد" (ward, literally "roses/flowers") for the crispy fried-onion topping is CONFIRMED by multiple Egyptian sources, but it is regional/old-downtown shop jargon, not universal — most menus and staff call the same topping "تقلية" (taʾleya) or simply "بصل" (basal); other counter code-words recorded at the old Koshary El Tahrir include "مونة" (sauce), "كهرمان" (lentils), "بندق" (rice) and "لوز" (macaroni).
- Portion sizes are ordered by NAME and by container: صغير/وسط/كبير (small/medium/large) or branded "علبة" (box) names; the second-helping top-up is "كمالة" (kemala); takeaway comes in an "علبة" (foil/plastic box) with the three sauces (صلصة/دقة/شطة) in SEPARATE small bags or sachets; payment is almost always pay-first at a cashier who gives you a ticket.

## Key Findings

**The three shop archetypes to model.** Egypt's koshary landscape is dominated by a handful of famous names, but they are visually and operationally distinct. Model three templates:
1. **The downtown legend** — a corner building given over to nothing but koshary, several floors, neon-lit facade, open assembly counter at street level, cashier desks on each floor. Abou Tarek and the original Koshary El Tahrir are the archetypes.
2. **The neighborhood/national chain** — cleaner, brighter, standardized branches with delivery, wall menu boards, fluorescent lighting, tiled/granite surfaces, foil-box takeaway. Sayed Hanafy, El Omda, Gad, Zezo, Tom & Basal, El Tahrir's newer franchise.
3. **The modern boutique** — air-conditioned, design-magazine interiors, English menus, squeeze-bottle sauces, handsome bowls, high prices. Zooba is the definitive example; Ward Koshary is the "gourmet" newcomer.

**Cultural stature.** Koshary was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity on 10 December 2025 as "Koshary, daily life dish and practices associated with it" (No. 02278) — Egypt's 11th listing and its first food entry, with Culture Minister Ahmed Fouad Hanno calling it "an integral part" of Egyptian identity. This matters for tone: the dish is treated as a national symbol, not just cheap fuel.

**The counter is a STRAIGHT line, not an L or semicircle.** The maestro stands (often on a slightly raised dais), holds a bowl in one hand, and flicks calculated scoops from adjoining stainless-steel vats with a long-handled metal spoon, banging the spoon on each pot rim — a full bowl is assembled in about five seconds. This clanging rhythm is culturally iconic ("the koshary symphony").

## Details

### 1) Famous koshary chains and shops

**Koshary Abou Tarek (كشري أبو طارق) — Cairo, the icon.** Founded by Youssef Zaki (known as "Abou Tarek," nicknamed "Malek El Koshary"/King of Koshary by a French newspaper) who began from a wooden street cart on the corner of Maarouf and Champollion streets in downtown Cairo. Sources date the cart to the 1930s–1950s; Zaki himself said in a firsthand interview that he bought the shop premises (a former Upper-Egyptian coffee shop) around 1989 and took three years to pay it off. Now a multi-storey landmark (four floors, with a fifth built) dedicated entirely to koshary. Serves ONLY koshary plus rice pudding/desserts. Signs read "We have no other branch" and "please pay before order." A theatrical, touristy, high-turnover operation. In January 2015, at the "Engezni" Koshary Festival in Zamalek's El Horreya Garden, a dish made with Abou Tarek set a Guinness World Record — about 10 meters wide, 1.20 meters high, weighing 8,000+ kg, enough to feed almost 14,000 people. Its media adviser Emad Yousery says it is recognized among TasteAtlas's "Best 100 Restaurants in the World," ranked (per one report) 64th. It has expanded to Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Kuwait. Reputation: the consensus "eat here first" spot, though many locals argue the quality-to-price ratio has slipped and it now targets tourists.

**Koshary El Tahrir (كشري التحرير) — Cairo, the connoisseur's pick.** The brand markets itself as "Authentic Egyptian Koshary served since 1963." Originally beside the American University's Greek Campus downtown, marked by an indigo-blue/white/red logo. Widely regarded by Egyptians as making the "best" bowl. NOTE a franchise split: the original (blue/red logo, few branches) versus a newer, larger franchise (red/orange/yellow/green logo) run by the founder's brother, now 50+ branches worldwide including Saudi Arabia, London (its first branch outside Egypt, at 175 Edgware Road, W2 2HR, opened 2023) and the USA. The newer branches are modern, spacious, air-conditioned ("you feel like you're in an airport"), some with food delivered to tables by a lift/elevator. Menu uses branded size names — the London menu lists King (£14.40), Large (£12), Top (£9.60) and Tahrir (£7.20), with add-ons (lentils/fried onions/chickpeas) at £2.40 each.

**Koshary Sayed Hanafy (كشري سيد حنفي) — Cairo, the local favourite.** Started 1952 as a humble cart, grew into a family-owned institution focused on production quality and price across all branches. Per a FindinEgypt listing, "the dining room is inviting with Versailles inspired details and walls covered with mirrors. There is an outdoor dining area overlooking the famous Orabi Square." Signature touches: a hot red-pepper chili oil and a wedge of lemon served with the bowl. Less touristy than Abou Tarek; many downtown-raised Cairenes quietly prefer it. Menu size names include علبة بيج ستار, لارج, ستار بلس.

**Koshary El Omda (كشري العمدة) — Cairo/Giza, "the village chief."** A well-known chain (branches across Cairo/6th October, plus Dubai and Jeddah) serving koshary plus tagines, pizza/fetteer, chicken and desserts. Some branches are "touristy" with live music and comfortable seating at higher prices; positions itself as family-friendly.

**Koshary Gad (جاد) — Cairo, the popular-food giant.** One of Egypt's biggest popular-food chains; koshary is one item among a full menu of Egyptian pizza/fetteer, sandwiches and dishes at cheap prices. Distinctly a fast-food-style chain rather than a koshary specialist.

**Koshary Zezo (كشري زيزو) — Giza/Cairo.** A reliable multi-branch operation (Haram/Bahr al-Aazam, Manial, Nasr City, Heliopolis) praised for consistent "standard" quality, reasonable prices, and — at most branches other than Manial — well-made fetteer and pizza. The Manial branch is noted for its "soul."

**Tom & Basal (طوم أند بصل) — Cairo.** A newer franchise known for generous lentil/chickpea toppings and cheap per-topping add-ons; also does pizza and fetteer; many branches open 24/7 with wide delivery hours.

**Other genuinely famous names.** Koshary Hind (كشري هند) — a long-established, low-key downtown old-timer with excellent traditional bowls. El Khedewy/Al-Khedive (الخديوي), a decades-old competitive institution. Koshary El Zaeem (الزعيم), El Embrator (الإمبراطور), Sheikh El Balad (Nasr City, celebrated koshary + fetteer), Abou Ramy, El Sheikh, El Sultan, El Sayed, and many local names built on "pasha/prince/chief" branding (El Basha, El Brince, El Omda, El Khedewy).

**Alexandria and elsewhere.** Alexandria's koshary is a distinct dish — "كشري إسكندراني" made with yellow (split) lentils, often no tomato sauce, and sometimes topped with fried boiled eggs. [Chez Nermine](https://cheznermine.com/2024/08/12/koshari-iskandarani/) Suez versions use yellow lentils and even shrimp. [Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/travel/the-layered-history-of-koshary-egypts-most-beloved-street-food-180988262/) Known Alexandria names include Koshary Ala El Sokhn (cited as the city's best) [Egyptphotographytours](https://www.egyptphotographytours.com/dining-out-in-alexandria) and yellow-lentil neighborhood shops; the legendary Mohamed Ahmed is famous for ful/falafel rather than koshary specifically. In Luxor, Al Zaeem ("The Revolutionary") is cited as the city's best, with a two-door layout (dine-in upstairs, takeaway at street level). [blogspot](http://traveltuesdays.blogspot.com/2011/03/al-zaeem-luxors-best-koushary.html)

**Modern reinventions.** Zooba (زوبا), founded in Cairo's Zamalek in 2012, took Egyptian street food upmarket: bright colors, geometric patterns, and a visual identity created by &Walsh (Jessica Walsh) for its New York launch, in collaboration with Cairo calligrapher Mohamed Mohamed ("Zizo") and inspired by "the hand-painted typography on foul carts, geometric patterned tapes, mix & matched coloured tiles"; interiors by Ahmed El Husseiny of AE Superlab (Brooklyn). It offers tiled/granite finishes, allergen-labelled English menus, whole-grain koshary options, and handsome bowls at 3–5× street prices; branches in Zamalek, Maadi, Korba (Heliopolis), New Cairo, City Stars, and a flagship inside the Grand Egyptian Museum. Ward Koshary repackages the dish for a younger, wealthy clientele with sleek squeeze-bottle sauces for influencers; upscale restaurants like Abou El Sid and Felfela serve refined table-service koshary as one item on a full menu.

### 2) The physical preparation station / counter (the most important section)

**Shape and orientation.** The assembly counter is a **long, straight line** ("assembly-line set-up"), not an L-shape or semicircle. The cook staff line up behind it; at high-volume shops like Abou Tarek several assemblers work side by side to keep up with the lunch flood. The maestro (الأسطى) typically stands, sometimes on a slightly **raised dais**, with the vats in front of him within arm's reach.

**Containers — all stainless steel / metal.** In front of the worker are "massive stainless steel vats" / "towering metal containers," one per component: the steaming rice-lentil-vermicelli mix (روز بشعرية), macaroni/pasta, chickpeas (حمص), and crispy fried onions (in an open tray for quick hand access). On street carts the carbs sit in round steel pots ("حلة"), and a cart classically has three pots (pasta; chickpeas; rice-lentil-vermicelli mix). The tomato sauce (صلصة) is kept hot in a separate pot and dispensed from metal pitchers and glass bottles. NOTE: traditional shops do NOT use a glass sneeze-guard, bain-marie display, or rectangular gastronorm trays — that is a modern/Western touch; the classic look is open steel vats on a hot counter (the counter surface itself is hot).

**Left-to-right ingredient order.** No source explicitly documents the exact left-to-right placement of vats, so treat any specific order as an informed reconstruction rather than a documented fact. What IS reliably attested is the **assembly sequence**, which maps onto the container line: (1) pasta/macaroni and the rice-lentil-vermicelli base go in first, (2) then chickpeas, (3) then tomato sauce poured over, (4) then a final flourish of crispy fried onions on top. [blogspot](http://traveltuesdays.blogspot.com/2011/03/al-zaeem-luxors-best-koushary.html) The garlic-vinegar (دقة) and chili (شطة) are NOT applied by the worker — they are left for the customer.

**The worker's motion.** He does NOT walk the counter — he stands in one spot and reaches. He holds the bowl/plate in one hand and, with a long-handled metal spoon in the other, flicks calculated scoops "with flair and rhythm," even tossing ingredients through the air into the bowl. After each scoop he smacks/bangs the spoon against the pot rim and bowl sides, producing the characteristic clang — the "koshary symphony." A full plate is assembled in about five seconds; experienced maestros describe the movements as muscle memory.

**Bowls and takeaway containers.** A stack of empty bowls/plates is kept within immediate arm's reach at his station (he "grabs a bowl" to start each order). Dine-in was traditionally served in aluminum/metal bowls; many shops shifted to plastic/melamine/disposable ware during and after COVID (a change some purists lament — "I want metal plates"). Takeaway goes into an "علبة" (ʿelba) — a round/oval plastic tub or foil container — sealed with a plastic spoon and the sauces bagged separately.

**Workflow: order → assembly → handoff → customer.** At busy shops the flow is: (1) customer goes to the **cashier/till first, states size, pays, and gets a ticket**; (2) hands the ticket to the maestro at the counter; (3) maestro assembles the bowl in seconds; (4) for dine-in a runner/waiter carries it to the table (larger shops have a "trolley boy" moving food from kitchen to counter, and waiters delivering to tables), or for takeaway a separate hand seals the box and bags the sauces. In smaller shops the same station handles both dine-in plating and takeaway boxing side by side.

### 3) Order size names and ordering terminology

- **صغير / soghayyar** = small; **وسط / wasat** = medium; **كبير / kebir** = large. This is the core spoken terminology at nearly every shop.
- Many shops sell by **container name rather than plain size**: **علبة / ʿelba** = box/tub (takeaway unit); e.g., Abou Tarek's menu ladder runs علبة صغير → علبة أبو طارق → علبة مخصوص ("special," extra everything) → علبة الأكيلة → علبة الجولدن مان → حلة أبو طارق وسط/كبير (a "حلة"/hilla = pot = family sharing size, often "سفري"/safari = takeaway-only). El Tahrir uses Large/Top/King (and a base "Tahrir" size); Sayed Hanafy uses Big Star/Large/Star Plus; other chains use بريمو/لارج/سوبر/ميجا/جامبو.
- **طبق / tabaʾ** = plate (dine-in); **صحن / sahn** = bowl/dish; **حلة / hilla** = pot (family size).
- **كمالة / kemala** = a small extra "top-up" helping for someone still hungry after the base plate — CONFIRMED as historic Koshary El Tahrir terminology [daraj](https://daraj.media/%D8%B1%D8%AD%D9%84%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%8F%D8%B4%D9%8E%D8%B1%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%82-%D8%B0%D9%88-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AE-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%BA/) and still seen on menus (e.g., "طبق كشري كمالة").
- Historically at some shops you didn't state a size at all — you told the cashier **how much money** you wanted to spend and the plate was dished accordingly.
- Add-ons are ordered by component name: **صلصة** (sauce), **تقلية** (fried onions), **دقة** (garlic-vinegar), **شطة** (chili), **حمص** (chickpeas), **عدس** (lentils), each available in small/medium add-on portions.

### 4) The separate sauce/topping packages and the term "ورد"

**Separate sauces — confirmed.** The tomato sauce (صلصة/salsa), garlic-vinegar dressing (دقة/daʾa — a mix of garlic, vinegar, cumin, lemon, salt, water) [blogspot](http://traveltuesdays.blogspot.com/2011/03/al-zaeem-luxors-best-koushary.html) and chili sauce (شطة/shatta — chili + oil, often fiery) are treated as customer-controlled condiments. For dine-in they sit ON the table in glass bottles / metal pitchers / small bowls (the vessel size hints at the intended proportion). [blogspot](http://traveltuesdays.blogspot.com/2011/03/al-zaeem-luxors-best-koushary.html) For takeaway they are given in SEPARATE small plastic bags or, in modern shops, branded sachets — never poured onto takeaway food, because they'd make it soggy. Abou Tarek's box names literally itemize the sauce bundle ("صلصة، تقلية، دقة، شطة"). Modern gentrified shops replace bottles/bags with "fancy packaging and logos on small sachets."

**"ورد" (ward) — verified but not universal.** Confirmed by a firsthand Egyptian account of old Koshary El Tahrir: the shop's in-house "language" called the crispy fried onions "ورد" (ward = roses/flowers), alongside other code-words — sauce = "مونة" (moona), lentils = "كهرمان" (kahraman/amber), rice = "بندق" (bondoʾ/hazelnut), macaroni = "لوز" (loz/almond), plus "المثلث" (the triangle) for a combined extra of lentils+onions+sauce, and "كمالة" for a top-up. [daraj](https://daraj.media/%D8%B1%D8%AD%D9%84%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%8F%D8%B4%D9%8E%D8%B1%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%82-%D8%B0%D9%88-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AE-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%BA/) A separate cooking source also explicitly equates "ورد الكشري" with the crispy fried onions ("البصل المقرمش"). IMPORTANT nuance for the game: "ورد" is old-downtown/insider slang and a poetic/marketing flourish (a brand "Ward Koshary" even trades on it); it is NOT the standard menu word. The everyday terms for the fried-onion topping are **"تقلية" (taʾleya)** and **"بصل" (basal)**. So model "ورد" as a piece of authentic staff flavour, not as the default UI label. Sources agree the onions are the single most important element ("the whole game").

**Other jargon and shouted order-calling.** The maestro/counterman shouts the order to a relay in the kitchen or lower floor; the old El Tahrir account describes a chain of "one who receives and relays, one who receives and relays again, one who executes" working entirely without pen or paper. [daraj](https://daraj.media/%D8%B1%D8%AD%D9%84%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%8F%D8%B4%D9%8E%D8%B1%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%82-%D8%B0%D9%88-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AE-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%BA/) Recorded shouted phrases are mostly warnings and banter rather than coded kitchen calls: at El Tahrir a server (Am Mesbah) roamed shouting "الشطة حامية نااار" ("the chili is fire!") and "ماترغش الشطة" ("don't shake the chili"); [daraj](https://daraj.media/%D8%B1%D8%AD%D9%84%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%8F%D8%B4%D9%8E%D8%B1%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%82-%D8%B0%D9%88-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AE-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%BA/) El Embrator waiters warned "الشطة نار، بجد مش هزار" and called every male customer "الباشا الجميل" and every woman "ست الكل." The worker himself is "الأسطى" (el-asta, the master). Note: specific coded kitchen-to-counter call-outs are largely undocumented in text — the game team should watch Abou Tarek YouTube footage for authentic audio.

### 5) Interior design and atmosphere

**Old traditional downtown shops.** Concrete multi-storey buildings; at night the facade blazes with hundreds of neon-colored lights [Bidoun](https://www.bidoun.org/articles/how-s-business-abou-tarek) (Abou Tarek's is famously lit blue and red). [Vianca That Vagabond](https://viancathatvagabond.com/egyptian-koshary-abou-tarek/) Inside: a glass partition/barrier at the entrance behind which the ingredient vats and stacks of plastic takeaway tubs are visible [daraj](https://daraj.media/%D8%B1%D8%AD%D9%84%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%8F%D8%B4%D9%8E%D8%B1%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%82-%D8%B0%D9%88-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AE-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%BA/) (the stacked tubs in the window are a visual signature that identifies a koshary shop). [blogspot](http://traveltuesdays.blogspot.com/2011/03/al-zaeem-luxors-best-koushary.html) Walls covered in mirrors and/or marble/granite veneer; tables topped with a silvery metal sheet; older floors were strewn with sawdust to absorb spills (swept periodically). [daraj](https://daraj.media/%D8%B1%D8%AD%D9%84%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%8F%D8%B4%D9%8E%D8%B1%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%82-%D8%B0%D9%88-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AE-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%BA/) Walls hung with framed photos of the founder, framed newspaper articles, and portraits in gilded/crystalline frames; a cash desk on each floor manned by the owner or a son. On each table: a metal water jug and metal cups, plus two bottles — one دقة (pale liquid), one شطة (fiery red) [daraj](https://daraj.media/%D8%B1%D8%AD%D9%84%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D9%8F%D8%B4%D9%8E%D8%B1%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%82-%D8%B0%D9%88-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AE-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%BA/) — and little trays of salt and cumin. Music: at Abou Tarek, the Shaaban Abdel Rahim song "I Love Koshary Abou Tarek" plays on loop. Ambience is loud, steamy, crowded, fast, communal (strangers share tables).

**Newer chain branches.** Brighter and cleaner — fluorescent/LED lighting, granite or marble countertops, tiled walls, illuminated signboards with strong spotlights to catch the eye, air conditioning, bathrooms, uniformed staff, wall-mounted menu boards, table service, delivery. The newer El Tahrir franchise branches feel "five-star," spacious and "like an airport," some with elevators bringing food up to tables. Tables carry the same condiment kit (vinegar/dakka/shatta bottles, salt, cumin, tissues).

**Modern boutique (Zooba/Ward).** Design-led: bright Egyptian-street-inspired color palettes, geometric patterned tiles, mix-and-matched colored tiles, custom Arabic typography, latticework (mashrabiya) screens, laser-etched steel tabletops, stone/granite cladding evoking medieval Cairo's "ablaq" striped stonework (at the GEM flagship), comfortable seating, English menus, and sleek squeeze bottles instead of glass dispensers.

### 6) Other operational detail

- **Staffing roles** (from an Egyptian small-business guide): cashier(s) who also manage the rice-pudding/soda fridge; the maestro/"عامل الوقوف على الأكل" who plates attractively to draw repeat custom, plus an assistant who hands plates to waiters or takeaway customers; a "trolley boy" moving cooked food from the kitchen to the front; waiters for the dining hall; kitchen cooks (3+); cleaners for the space and the tables; and a bookkeeper. [entej](https://blogs.entej.com/blog/71/%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9-%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84-%D9%83%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%8A/) All in a unified uniform.
- **Two spaces:** a back kitchen/storeroom where everything is pre-cooked and mixes are made (large gas cylinders, big pots/"اذان"), and the front shop where food is kept warm in open vats and plated. [entej](https://blogs.entej.com/blog/71/%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9-%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84-%D9%83%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%8A/) Ingredients arrive via supply contracts for pasta/rice sacks, tomatoes/onions, lentils/chickpeas, and soft/mineral water. [entej](https://blogs.entej.com/blog/71/%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9-%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84-%D9%83%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%8A/)
- **Payment:** pay-first at a cashier who issues a ticket you hand to the counter; larger modern shops also accept Visa; carts and tiny shops are cash-only.
- **Takeaway packaging:** round/oval plastic tubs or foil "علب" sealed with a plastic spoon; sauces in separate small bags/sachets; [blogspot](http://traveltuesdays.blogspot.com/2011/03/al-zaeem-luxors-best-koushary.html) onions sometimes bagged separately for delivery so they stay crisp.
- **Delivery:** widespread at newer chains (hotlines and the big Egyptian delivery apps); the original downtown legends historically did NOT deliver.
- **Peak hours:** lunch rush is the flood (office workers, students); downtown shops are also busy late at night and when families stroll downtown in summer; many chains open ~9–10am to 1–2am, some 24/7.
- **Pricing (mid-2026, ~50 EGP to the USD):** neighborhood cart ~20–40 EGP; traditional shop ~25–80 EGP; modern boutique (Zooba) ~90–150 EGP; upscale restaurant ~120–250 EGP; family takeaway ~150–300 EGP. A small chain plate is roughly $0.60. Prices have risen sharply with inflation and koshary is used by Egyptians as an informal inflation gauge.
- **Speed/rhythm:** food arrives in under ~5 minutes even when packed; [Vianca That Vagabond](https://viancathatvagabond.com/egyptian-koshary-abou-tarek/) individual bowls assembled in ~5 seconds; high table turnover; Cairenes "inhale" a bowl in about four minutes.

## Recommendations

For the game redesign, prioritize in this order:
1. **Build the core loop around the straight steel counter and the flick-and-clang rhythm.** The maestro stands fixed, bowl in one hand, long spoon in the other, scooping base carbs → chickpeas → sauce → onions in sequence, banging the spoon on pot rims each time. This is the single most recognizable, "gamifiable" ritual. Benchmark: a real bowl is built in ~5 seconds.
2. **Model the pay-first workflow as a two-station flow:** cashier (choose size, get ticket) → counter (maestro assembles) → table/takeaway. This is authentic and gives natural game "stations."
3. **Use authentic size/ordering vocabulary in the UI:** صغير/وسط/كبير and علبة/طبق/حلة, with "كمالة" as a top-up mechanic and per-component add-ons (صلصة/تقلية/دقة/شطة/حمص/عدس).

## Caveats
- **Left-to-right container order is not documented in text sources** — only the scooping sequence (carbs → chickpeas → sauce → onions on top) is reliable. Verify against video before locking art.
- **"ورد" for fried onions is verified but regional/insider slang**, not the standard menu term; sources overwhelmingly use "تقلية" or "بصل." Present it as flavour, not fact-of-universal-usage.
- **Founding dates conflict:** Abou Tarek's cart is variously dated 1930s/1945/1950, with the permanent shop ~1989; Koshary El Tahrir markets itself as "since 1963." Treat "since the mid-20th century" as safe.
- **Metal vs. plastic serviceware** is in flux — traditionally aluminum/metal, widely shifted to disposable post-COVID; both coexist today.
- **Shouted kitchen call-outs** are thinly documented in text; recorded phrases are mostly chili warnings and customer banter. Watch footage for authentic audio.
- Alexandria/Suez koshary differs from Cairo (yellow lentils, eggs, sometimes shrimp, often no tomato sauce) — if your game is "Cairo koshary," don't mix these in.
- Some cited price/menu figures come from aggregator/menu sites of varying reliability; treat exact EGP figures as approximate and time-sensitive given rapid inflation.
